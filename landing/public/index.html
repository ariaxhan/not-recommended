01 / Start with a question

The homepage is gone. In its place: a question box.

You type what you actually want to know, and you go find it. Your questions become threads you can return to — a record of curiosity instead of a history of impulses. Radical, we know.

02 / Today's detours

Three random Wikipedia articles a day. Not personalized. That's the point.

Real randomness, drawn from the whole encyclopedia. Not ranked, not optimized, not "because you watched." Serendipity that no engagement metric chose for you. Reroll all three if today's draw is a dud — but you reroll the set, not the slot.

03 / The quiet parts

Shorts: hidden. Sidebar recommendations: gone. Comments: off by default.

Everything engineered to make one video become eleven is starved out. When a video ends, you get deliberate exits — go deeper, challenge this, cross disciplines, or end the session — instead of a countdown to the next thing.

04 / The why gate

It asks “why?” before a video plays.

Annoying? Only if you don't have an answer. If you do, it takes two seconds and you watch with a purpose. If you don't — that pause just saved you an hour.

05 / What it will never do

Built out of spite for the attention economy.

  • backend
  • analytics
  • AI
  • ads
  • telemetry
  • remote code
  • dark patterns

Your data stays in your browser because it never leaves. The only network request goes to Wikipedia, and it doesn't carry your name.
